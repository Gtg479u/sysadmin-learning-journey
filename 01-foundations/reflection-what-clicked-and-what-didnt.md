# Reflection: What Clicked and What Didn’t (Active Directory Foundations)

## Getting Started
A lot of this wasn’t completely new to me. I’ve worked with virtual machines and also AD before at work and in a bootcamp, and I’ve heard the terminology used in real environments.

The difference this time was actually building everything myself and connecting the dots. Instead of just hearing the terms or following along, I had to understand how everything worked together and what was happening behind the scenes.

## Why I Started This Project
I started this project to gain real, hands-on experience with system administration so I can be ready when an opportunity comes up. I didn’t want to just wait to be taught or wait for the “right time.” I wanted to take initiative, build something on my own, and start developing real confidence with these skills.

## What Confused Me
At the beginning, the biggest challenge was understanding the “why” behind the setup. I could follow steps, but I didn’t always understand how everything was connected or why certain configurations mattered.

## What Frustrated Me
The most frustrating part was getting the domain controller to communicate with GT01. I spent a lot of time troubleshooting and going in circles before realizing it was a DNS and IP configuration issue. It was frustrating in the moment, but it also helped everything start to make more sense.

## What Finally Clicked
What really clicked for me is that this kind of learning takes time. I can’t rush it. Things started to make more sense once I slowed down, paid attention to what was happening, and worked through the problems instead of just trying to get to the end.

## Looking Ahead
I want to keep building on this foundation and start making stronger connections to what I see at work. The goal is to become more confident, both technically and in conversations with the team, and continue moving toward a system administrator role.
